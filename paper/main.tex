\section{Introduction}

Embedding-based retrieval aims to recall a match set of semantically relevant documents satisfying the user's query.
Such embeddings are often used in retrieval tasks, such as RAG, product search, and image search, where fine-grained matching of details between the query and the document determine relevance.

Much of this progress is due to broadly applicable techniques, such as instruction tuning and chain of thought.
However, these strategies are not applicable to embedding models.
Combined with the parameter count, representational constraints, and document corpus size on embedding models, this results in a significant language modeling gap between embedding models and generative models; it is common for embedding models to erroneously retrieve documents that a similarly sized generative model can trivially identify as irrelevant.
Thus, we seek a method for distilling the relevant categorization capabilities of a generative model directly into and embedding model.

Specifically, we concentrate on improving conjuntive queries --- those seeking documents that \textbf{have} attributes a, b, c ... and \textbf{do not have} attributes x, y, z, ... . 
This focus is motivated by patterns in user queries and the design of pre-existing non-embedding search systems.
For example, the conjunctive filtering paradigm already exists in product search in the form of attribute filters on e-commerce sites, such as ``screen size $>30$\,cm'' for laptops.
Users find such categories useful both in searching for and understanding products. 
Unfortunately, attribute ontologies are usually fixed, failing when a user desires a product with or without a novel attribute.
Because unique and novel attributes differentiate products, sellers are well motivated to include them in product descriptions to differentiate their products. 
As a result, natural language search systems, such as dense retrieval, are better suited for the long tail of highly specific queries.
Similar use cases exist other retrieval tasks, including for images, videos, resumes, patents, and real estate.

% mechanics - why our system works

Unfortunately, publicly available retrieval datasets, such as \red{cite} mainly contain simple queries, with little to no conjunction or negation.
Instead, datasets like the popular ECSI \red{cite} focus on providing hard negatives---similar pairs of similar documents, only one of which is valid.
This neglects an important dynamic in the user experience of search systems wherein queries tend to gain attributes over time as users refine them. 
\citet{hirsch2020query} find that over 50\% of queries on Ebay occur during a reformulation session, with queries growing in length over time.
Longer queries were associated with increased purchase rates, indicating that support for highly specific queries should be a priority.

There is evidence to believe that queries are underspecified because users doubt the capabilities of recommender systems \citep{kato2014cognitive}.
\citet{papenmeier2020modern} find that the length of users' free-form text descriptions of their desired laptops include on average 29 words and 3.83 attributes. 
However, when Amazon product search, they entered only 2.2 words, indicating that users do not feel including additional attributes would be helpful. 

Users' pessimism is not unfounded, as bi-encoders have known limitations in their expressivity.
% \citet{zhang2025excluir} and \citet{weller2024nevir} show that embedding models struggle to model negation.
These include negation \citep{zhang2025excluir, weller2024nevir}, set operations \citep{killingback2025benchmarking}, constraint dilution \citep{lu2025multiconir}, and attribute binding \citep{yuksekgonul2022and}.
It then follows that user query behavior may suffer from a chicken-and-egg problem; users do not use complex queries because they expect models to fail, while practicioners to do not optimize for complex queries because they do not appear in user data.

To address this gap, we introduce Ours, a strategy for training strong embedding models for conjunctive queries.
We first use an LLM to extract attributes of each document in structured form, creating a large, synthetic dataset with LLM-level accuracy.
We then use a Hamming distance-based loss function to train the embedding model.
This contrasts with baseline loss functions that treat all negatives as equally wrong.
For the query ``warm red hoodie'', the document ``warm blue jacket'' is considered just as incorrect as ``gas-powered lawnmower''.
On the other hand, Ours sets the loss proportional to the squared number of attributes that must be changed to satisfy the query, and much higher if the subject (hoodie vs. lawnmower) must be changed.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{Dense retrieval and graded supervision.}
Dense bi-encoders enable efficient first-stage retrieval by mapping queries and candidates into a shared embedding space~\cite{reimers-gurevych-2019-sentence,karpukhin-etal-2020-dense}.
Their effectiveness, however, depends strongly on the selection and supervision of negative examples.
Methods such as ANCE and RocketQA improve training by retrieving challenging negatives, sharing negatives across batches, or denoising mined candidates~\cite{xiong2020approximate,qu-etal-2021-rocketqa}.
Other approaches move beyond uniform positive--negative separation by distilling continuous teacher-score differences, as in MarginMSE and TAS-Balanced~\cite{hofstatter2020improving,hofstatter2021tas}, or by using relevance- and attribute-dependent margins in metric-learning objectives~\cite{zhou2020ladder,Jeong_2021_ICCV,Zhao2019}.
These methods demonstrate the value of informative negatives and graded training signals, but generally derive the degree of separation from teacher scores, relevance labels, or broad semantic relationships.
In contrast, we derive supervision from the discrete structure of the query: candidates are graded by the number of query-relevant feature edits required to make them satisfy its constraints.

\paragraph{Compositional, Boolean, and product retrieval.}
Product relevance is often compositional, as a relevant item may need to satisfy multiple positive and negative attribute constraints simultaneously.
Prior product-search methods address this problem through behaviorally informed negative sampling or representations that explicitly model product facets and attribute composition~\cite{Mohan2019,kong2022multiaspect,Choudhary2022}.
A related line of work studies logical composition in dense retrieval.
QUEST considers natural-language queries representing set operations, while BoolQuestions examines conjunction, disjunction, and exclusion through Boolean-aware training and query decomposition~\cite{malaviya-etal-2023-quest,zhang-etal-2024-boolquestions}.
Logical query-embedding methods such as BetaE and ConE instead construct geometric representations that directly support logical operators~\cite{NEURIPS2020_e43739bb,NEURIPS2021_a0160709}.

Negation is particularly challenging for embedding-based retrieval.
NevIR shows that bi-encoders are often insensitive to negated meaning, while product-search systems have mitigated related failures through explicit detection and filtering of prohibited attributes~\cite{weller-etal-2024-nevir,Merra2023}.
Collectively, these findings expose a limitation of representations dominated by aggregate semantic similarity: candidates may appear highly similar to a query despite violating one or more of its essential constraints.
Our objective directly supervises this distinction by organizing candidates according to their degree of constraint satisfaction.

\paragraph{Synthetic supervision for retrieval.}
Large language models have increasingly been used to generate targeted supervision for dense retrieval.
Syntriever and SyNeg generate queries, relevant examples, or challenging negatives that expose distinctions absent from conventional training data~\cite{kim-baek-2025-syntriever,li2024syneg}.
In product retrieval, Agapaki and Gill combine structured product information with LLM-based relevance assessment to identify difficult training examples~\cite{agapaki2026negative}.
These approaches show that synthetic data can control which distinctions a retriever encounters during training.
Our use of generated information differs in an important respect: generated feature annotations specify the candidate structure, but do not directly determine the supervision target.
Given these annotations and the query constraints, the target distance is computed deterministically as a discrete feature-edit distance.

\paragraph{Multimodal compositional retrieval.}
Compositional constraints create analogous difficulties in vision--language retrieval.
CLIP learns a shared embedding space for images and text, whereas the CIRR benchmark and methods such as CLIP4CIR study retrieval from queries that combine a reference image with a textual modification~\cite{pmlr-v139-radford21a,Liu_2021_ICCV,Baldrati_2022_CVPR}.
More recent approaches introduce supervision specifically for negation and fine-grained composition.
CoN-CLIP and NegationCLIP improve sensitivity to negated concepts, while CoLLM uses LLM-generated triplets for composed image retrieval~\cite{Singh_2025_WACV,Park_2025_ICCV,Huynh_2025_CVPR}.
This literature further demonstrates that general semantic alignment does not reliably encode whether each positive and negative constraint is satisfied.

\paragraph{Positioning of our work.}
Prior research has improved compositional retrieval through hard-negative mining, teacher-derived graded supervision, Boolean-aware modeling, attribute-sensitive representations, and synthetic example generation.
Our work connects these directions by introducing a discrete, query-relative structural distance: the minimum number of query-relevant feature edits required for a candidate to satisfy the query.
Unlike teacher scores or learned relevance estimates, this distance is deterministic once the query constraints and candidate features are specified.
We use it as graded supervision for first-stage retrieval, encouraging distances in the learned embedding space to reflect degree of constraint satisfaction.
We evaluate the resulting formulation in both textual and image-based product retrieval.

\subsection{Background}

Traditional Margin-MSE loss \citep{hofstatter2020improving} using inner product similarity is defined as

% \begin{equation}
%     \mathcal{L}{q, x^+, x^-} = \Big( \inner{f(q)}{f(x^+)} - \inner{f(q)}{f(x^-)} \Big)^2
% \end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:trad-mse}
    \mathcal{L}(q, x^+, x^-) = \Big( \inner{f(q)}{f(x^+) - f(x^-)} - y \Big)^2 .
\end{equation}

Where $q$ is a query, $x^+$ is a relevant document, $x^-$ is a less relevant document, and $y$ is a scalar representing the margin between their relevance as determined by a teacher model.
$f$ is a function mapping queries or documents to their embeddings.

Formally, following \citep{lu2025multiconir}, let query $q_k$ be a query composed of $k$ conditions $C=\{c_1, c_2, ..., c_k\}$ with $k \in \{1, ..., \red{K}\}$.
We say a document $x$ is a positive document, $x^+$, with respect to $q_k$ if $x^+ \models C$, i.e., $x^+$ satisfies all $k$ conditions.
Otherwise, we say the document $x^-$ is negative with respect to $q_k$.


\subsection{Attribute Hamming Loss Function}

We introduce Attribute Hamming Loss, which is a form of Margin-MSE loss designed to distil knowledge from generative models rather than teacher embedding models.
Attribute Hamming Loss replaces the distillation target $y$ with an integer representing the number of constraints in the query violated by $x^-$, rescaled by a constant $V$.
% Training operates on triples $(q_k, x^+, x^-)$ in which the positive satisfies every condition of the query, and the predicted margin between the positive and negative similarities is regressed onto the number of query constraints the negative violates:

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:hamming}
    \mathcal{L}(q_k, x^+, x^-) = \Big( \inner{f(q_k)}{f(x^+) - f(x^-)} - \tfrac{1}{V} \underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{k} \ind{x^- \not\models c_i}}_{\text{\# of constraints violated by } x^-} \Big)^2 ,
\end{equation}

In Section \ref{???} we describe a data synthesis pipeline to construct training triples with known target values of $\sum_{i=1}^{k} \ind{x^- \not\models c_i}$.
The target value is zero when the negative satisfies every constraint and grows by $1/V$ per violated constraint.
This contrasts with binary classification training regimes, such as traditional triplet loss, where examples are either strictly correct or incorrect.

One can imagine this loss function attempting to approximating bit-wise filtering on a table of $n$ documents and arbitrarily many columns of attributes. 
Because for any given query $q_k$, only the $k$ attributes present in the query are relevant, the space requirement of this dataset can be bounded by $\mathcal{O}(nk)$.

\subsection{Dataset Generation}

We target two domains, e-commerce products and images, to demonstrate the multimodal capabilities of our system.
For e-commerce, we choose the ESCI corpus of Amazon products \red{cite} due to its large size.
For images, we choose the DeepFashion In-Shop because it contains images with subtle but objectively different attributes.

In both datasets, we select easy negatives through random sampling of the entire dataset.
We select candidates for hard negatives in ESCI using the pre-existing substitution labels.
For DeepFashion, we select a random example from the same category, such as ``blouses'' or ``graphic tees''.

To extract document attributes, we prompt Gemini 2.5 Flash with the pair of examples, using the prompt of Appendix~\ref{prompt:text-features} for products and Appendix~\ref{prompt:image-features} for images.
In the DeepFashion dataset, we include images of both products in the prompt, instructing the model not to generate attributes that are not visible in the images.

We instruct it to generate up to $k/4=5$ attributes that apply to only the first example, only the second example, to both examples, or to neither example, for a total of $k=20$ attributes.
We then select a random subset of these attributes to compose the query, negating attributes that do not apply to the positive example, ensuring that at least one attribute is not satisfied by the hard negative example.
This downsampling step is useful to ensure that selected attributes are not merely the most salient.
We manually annotate \red{xx} attributes across \red{xx} products in each dataset and find that the attributes are correct \red{xx\%} and \red{xx\%} of the time in ECSI and DeepFashion, respectively.
Thus, we obtain a set of $k$ attributes, all of which are satisfied by the positive example and $k'$ of which are \emph{not} satisfied by the hard negative example, where $0 \leq k' < k$.
We construct the query with the template "I am looking for: "{item}" that has: {postive + common attributes}; and does not have: {negative + neither attributes}".
Additionally, we construct a rewritten version of the query to test robustness to rephrasing and non-exact match, prompting the model to preserve intent and numeric values while replacing most attribute phrases with synonyms (Appendix~\ref{prompt:rephrase}).

We characterize the optimum of the attribute Hamming objective.
For a training triple the label equals a signed difference of attribute values, $y = \sum_{i \in C} s_i ( z_i(x^+) - z_i(x^-) )$, so a query representation formed as the signed sum of per-attribute probes matches the regression target termwise.
The correspondence holds in both directions: if every queried attribute is linearly recoverable from the document embeddings at error $\epsilon$, the query vector $\sum_{i \in C} s_i b_i$ attains loss below $(2k\epsilon)^2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:forward}); conversely, if the query family is closed under deleting a condition, a model with loss below $\delta^2$ on every triple yields a probe recovering each queried attribute at error $2\delta$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:delete}).
Under these hypotheses, minimizing the objective and making the query embedding a simultaneous linear probe for the attributes it names are the same requirement.
Triplet and in-batch objectives constrain only the ordering of scores and are satisfied by any embedding that ranks the positive first by at least the margin; constraining the margin's magnitude to a count makes partial constraint satisfaction a graded, linearly decodable quantity.
Under the additional assumption that document embeddings satisfy the linear representation hypothesis with $k'$-sparse attribute vectors, the construction of \citet{garg2026many} realizes loss $\delta^2$ at dimension $d = O(k'^2 k^2 \delta^{-2} \log m)$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:capacity}).
The dependence on the number of attributes is logarithmic, so a fixed-width embedding supports exponentially many.

\subsection{Setup}

\begin{definition}[Attributes and conditions]
\label{def:attr}
Let $L$ be the set of texts, comprising both queries and documents, and let $z_i : L \rightarrow \{0,1\}$ for $i \in [m]$ be attribute indicators, with $z_i(x) = 1$ when $x$ has attribute $i$.
A \emph{condition} $c_i$ names an attribute $i$ together with a \emph{polarity} $s_i \in \{-1, +1\}$.
A document $x$ \emph{satisfies} $c_i$, written $x \models c_i$, when $z_i(x) = 1$ in the case $s_i = +1$, and when $z_i(x) = 0$ in the case $s_i = -1$.
A query $q_C$ carries a set of conditions $C$ with $|C| = k$, and $x \models C$ means $x \models c_i$ for every $i \in C$.
\end{definition}

Note that $s_i^2 = |s_i| = 1$, used repeatedly below.

\begin{definition}[Embedding and objective]
\label{def:obj}
Let $f : L \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$ assign a representation to each text.
Training consumes triples $(q_C, x^+, x^-)$ in which $x^+ \models C$ and $x^-$ is a hard negative.
The model predicts the \emph{margin}, $M$, and is regressed onto the \emph{label}, $y$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-obj}
    M(q_C, x^+, x^-) = \inner{f(q_C)}{f(x^+) - f(x^-)} ,
    \qquad
    y(q_C, x^-) = \sum_{i \in C} \ind{x^- \not\models c_i} ,
\end{equation}
under the squared loss $\mathcal{L}(q_C, x^+, x^-) = \big( M(q_C, x^+, x^-) - y(q_C, x^-) \big)^2$.
\end{definition}

The trainer in fact regresses onto $y / V$ for a fixed constant $V > 0$.
The change of variables $g = V f(q_C)$ turns the $V$-scaled loss into $V^{-2} \big( \inner{g}{\Delta f} - y \big)^2$, so the two objectives share minimizers and we set $V = 1$ throughout.
Under a unit-norm constraint this change of variables is unavailable.
\red{Remark~\ref{rem:unitnorm} handles the unit-norm case.}

\begin{definition}[Linear recovery, after \citealp{garg2026many}]
\label{def:rec}
Let $S \subseteq L$ and $\epsilon > 0$.
Attribute $z_i$ is \emph{$(\epsilon, S)$-linearly recovered} by a probe $(b_i, \beta_i) \in \mathbb{R}^d \times \mathbb{R}$ if
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-rec}
    \big| \inner{b_i}{f(x)} + \beta_i - z_i(x) \big| < \epsilon
    \qquad \text{for all } x \in S .
\end{equation}
We write $r_i(x) = \inner{b_i}{f(x)} + \beta_i - z_i(x)$ for the \emph{residual}, so that Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-rec} reads $|r_i(x)| < \epsilon$ on $S$.
\citet{garg2026many} state linear accessibility with a strictly linear probe, $g_i(x) = \inner{b_i}{x}$; we allow the offset $\beta_i$, which is the usual linear probe with bias and is exactly linear recovery of $z_i$ from the homogeneous lift $(f(x), 1) \in \mathbb{R}^{d+1}$.
On the upper-bound side the offset is free, since affine probes contain linear ones (take $\beta_i = 0$) and existence results transfer verbatim.
On the lower-bound side, their Theorem 12 extends the dimension lower bound to binary-valued features, and its Corollary 13 shows that a bias and a monotonic activation do not raise the asymptotic capacity, so allowing $\beta_i$ does not let the model evade the bound; the binary restriction of our attributes is settled by the same theorem.
\end{definition}

Throughout, $S$ is the set of documents appearing in training triples, and we abbreviate
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deltas}
    \Delta f = f(x^+) - f(x^-) ,
    \qquad
    \Delta z_i = z_i(x^+) - z_i(x^-) ,
    \qquad
    \Delta r_i = r_i(x^+) - r_i(x^-) .
\end{equation}

\subsection{Four lemmas}

The first lemma replaces the case split of Definition~\ref{def:attr} by a single arithmetic expression, which is what allows positive and negated conditions to be handled together.

\begin{lemma}[Polarity identity]
\label{lem:polarity}
For every $x \in L$ and every condition $c_i$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-polarity}
    \ind{x \not\models c_i} = \tfrac{1}{2}\big( 1 - s_i ( 2 z_i(x) - 1 ) \big) .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Both sides take values in $\{0,1\}$, so it suffices to check the four combinations of $s_i$ and $z_i(x)$.
If $s_i = +1$ the right-hand side is $\tfrac{1}{2}(1 - 2z_i(x) + 1) = 1 - z_i(x)$, which is $0$ when $z_i(x) = 1$ and $1$ when $z_i(x) = 0$; by Definition~\ref{def:attr} a positive condition is satisfied exactly when $z_i(x) = 1$, so this agrees with the left-hand side in both cases.
If $s_i = -1$ the right-hand side is $\tfrac{1}{2}(1 + 2z_i(x) - 1) = z_i(x)$, which is $0$ when $z_i(x) = 0$ and $1$ when $z_i(x) = 1$; a negated condition is satisfied exactly when $z_i(x) = 0$, so again the two sides agree.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Attribute values of the positive]
\label{lem:pos}
If $x^+ \models C$, then $z_i(x^+) = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + s_i)$ for every $i \in C$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $i \in C$.
Since $x^+ \models c_i$ we have $\ind{x^+ \not\models c_i} = 0$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:polarity} gives $\tfrac{1}{2}(1 - s_i(2 z_i(x^+) - 1)) = 0$, that is, $s_i (2 z_i(x^+) - 1) = 1$.
Multiplying both sides by $s_i$ and using $s_i^2 = 1$ yields $2 z_i(x^+) - 1 = s_i$, and solving for $z_i(x^+)$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

% The value $\tfrac{1}{2}(1 + s_i)$ is $1$ for a positive condition and $0$ for a negated one, as it must be: the positive document has every attribute the query requires and none it forbids.

The next lemma is the pivot of the whole argument.
The label of Definition~\ref{def:obj} is a count computed from $x^-$ alone, yet the margin it is regressed onto is antisymmetric in $x^+$ and $x^-$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:label} shows the two are nonetheless compatible, by rewriting the count as a signed difference.

\begin{lemma}[Antisymmetric form of the label]
\label{lem:label}
If $x^+ \models C$, then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-label}
    y(q_C, x^-) = \sum_{i \in C} s_i \, \Delta z_i .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $i \in C$.
Expanding Lemma~\ref{lem:polarity} at $x^-$ and collecting terms,
\begin{equation}
    \ind{x^- \not\models c_i}
    = \tfrac{1}{2}\big( 1 - s_i(2 z_i(x^-) - 1) \big)
    = \tfrac{1}{2}(1 + s_i) - s_i z_i(x^-) .
\end{equation}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:pos} the leading term is exactly $z_i(x^+)$, and multiplying it by $s_i^2 = 1$ lets us write it as $s_i z_i(x^+)$.
Hence
\begin{equation}
    \ind{x^- \not\models c_i} = s_i z_i(x^+) - s_i z_i(x^-) = s_i \, \Delta z_i .
\end{equation}
Summing over $i \in C$ and applying Definition~\ref{def:obj} gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-label}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
\label{rem:constant}
The rewriting in Lemma~\ref{lem:label} is not cosmetic.
Written in terms of $x^-$ alone the label is $y = \tfrac{1}{2}\big(k + \sum_{i \in C} s_i\big) - \sum_{i \in C} s_i z_i(x^-)$, whose leading term is a nonzero constant.
A margin is antisymmetric, $M(q_C, x, x) = 0$, and so can never reproduce a constant; it is only because $x^+ \models C$ forces that constant to equal $\sum_{i \in C} s_i z_i(x^+)$ that the label takes a form a margin can match.
This is also why the objective of Eq.~\eqref{eq:hamming} is stated over triples rather than over single documents.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Margin decomposition]
\label{lem:decomp}
Let $b_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ for $i \in C$ and set $f(q_C) = \sum_{i \in C} s_i b_i$.
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-decomp}
    M(q_C, x^+, x^-) = \sum_{i \in C} s_i \Big( \inner{b_i}{f(x^+)} - \inner{b_i}{f(x^-)} \Big) .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The inner product is bilinear, hence linear in its first argument: $\inner{\sum_{i \in C} s_i b_i}{\Delta f} = \sum_{i \in C} s_i \inner{b_i}{\Delta f}$.
It is also linear in its second argument, so $\inner{b_i}{\Delta f} = \inner{b_i}{f(x^+)} - \inner{b_i}{f(x^-)}$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:deltas}.
Substituting the second identity into the first gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-decomp}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Main results}

The forward direction constructs the query representation explicitly; it is an existence statement, and whether training produces this vector is a question about optimization rather than about capacity.
The constructed vector need not be unit norm.

\begin{theorem}[Recovery implies low loss]
\label{thm:forward}
Let $S \subseteq L$ and $\epsilon > 0$, and suppose every attribute $z_i$ with $i \in C$ is $(\epsilon, S)$-linearly recovered by a probe $(b_i, \beta_i)$.
Then the query representation $f(q_C) = \sum_{i \in C} s_i b_i$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}(q_C, x^+, x^-) < (2 k \epsilon)^2
\end{equation}
for every triple with $x^+, x^- \in S$ and $x^+ \models C$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:decomp} the margin decomposes over conditions, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:label} so does the label.
Subtracting them termwise,
\begin{equation}
    M - y
    = \sum_{i \in C} s_i \Big( \big( \inner{b_i}{f(x^+)} - \inner{b_i}{f(x^-)} \big) - \Delta z_i \Big) .
\end{equation}
Regrouping each summand by document rather than by quantity, and using $\Delta z_i = z_i(x^+) - z_i(x^-)$,
\begin{equation}
    M - y
    = \sum_{i \in C} s_i \Big( \big( \inner{b_i}{f(x^+)} - z_i(x^+) \big) - \big( \inner{b_i}{f(x^-)} - z_i(x^-) \big) \Big)
    = \sum_{i \in C} s_i \, \Delta r_i ,
\end{equation}
by Definition~\ref{def:rec}, the offsets $\beta_i$ cancelling because each appears once at $x^+$ and once at $x^-$.
The error of the objective is therefore exactly a signed sum of differences of probe residuals.
Applying the triangle inequality to the sum, then $|s_i \Delta r_i| = |s_i| \, |\Delta r_i| = |\Delta r_i|$, and then the triangle inequality again inside each term,
\begin{equation}
    |M - y|
    \;\le\; \sum_{i \in C} \big| \Delta r_i \big|
    \;\le\; \sum_{i \in C} \Big( \big| r_i(x^+) \big| + \big| r_i(x^-) \big| \Big)
    \;<\; \sum_{i \in C} 2\epsilon
    \;=\; 2 k \epsilon ,
\end{equation}
where the strict inequality applies Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-rec} once at $x^+$ and once at $x^-$ for each of the $k$ conditions, both being members of $S$.
Squaring preserves the inequality for nonnegative quantities, giving $\mathcal{L} = (M - y)^2 < (2k\epsilon)^2$.
\end{proof}

Combining Theorem~\ref{thm:forward} with the construction of \citet{garg2026many} bounds the dimension required to realize the objective.
Their theorem assumes linear representation, $f(x) = A z(x)$ with sparse attribute vectors, which is stronger than the linear accessibility used above; we adopt it as a hypothesis.
The corollary is therefore an existence result about representations, not a property of the trained encoder.

\begin{corollary}[Conditional capacity]
\label{cor:capacity}
Assume additionally that document embeddings are linearly represented, $f(x) = A z(x)$ with $A \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times m}$ drawn as in the random construction of \citet{garg2026many}, and that the attribute vector $z(x) \in \{0,1\}^m$ of every $x \in S$ has at most $k'$ nonzero entries.
Their upper bound is stated as $d = O_\epsilon(k'^2 \log m)$; the random construction proving it supplies, for any $\epsilon > 0$, probes attaining Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-rec} once $d = O(k'^2 \epsilon^{-2} \log m)$, the $\epsilon^{-2}$ entering through the incoherence $\mu \approx \epsilon / k'$ of the random directions.
Taking $\epsilon = \delta / 2k$ and applying Theorem~\ref{thm:forward}, margin loss below $\delta^2$ on every $k$-condition query over these documents is realizable once
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-capacity}
    d = O\!\left( \frac{k'^2 k^2}{\delta^2} \, \log m \right) ,
    \qquad \text{equivalently} \qquad
    m = \exp\!\left( \Omega\!\left( \frac{d \, \delta^2}{k^2 k'^2} \right) \right) .
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

The dependence on the number of attributes is logarithmic.
The polynomial factor arises because Theorem~\ref{thm:forward} requires per-probe error $\epsilon = \delta / 2k$, while the $d = O_\epsilon(k^2 \log m)$ form of \citet{garg2026many} holds at fixed $\epsilon$.

The converse recovers probes from a trained model in two steps: differencing two queries isolates a single attribute, and a single positive document then fixes the offset.

\begin{theorem}[Low loss implies aggregate recovery]
\label{thm:back}
Let $\delta > 0$ and suppose $\mathcal{L}(q_C, x^+, x^-) < \delta^2$ for every triple with $x^+, x^- \in S$ and $x^+ \models C$.
Then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-agg}
    \Big| \inner{f(q_C)}{\Delta f} - \sum_{i \in C} s_i \, \Delta z_i \Big| < \delta
\end{equation}
for every such triple.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Both $\mathcal{L}$ and $\delta^2$ are nonnegative, so $\mathcal{L} < \delta^2$ is equivalent to $|M - y| < \delta$.
Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-obj} for $M$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:label} for $y$ gives Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-agg}.
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:back} constrains only the aggregate $\sum_{i \in C} s_i \Delta z_i$: a model could err on one condition and compensate on another without violating it.
Isolating a single attribute requires comparing two queries that differ in exactly one condition, which is the content of the following corollary.

\begin{corollary}[Per-attribute recovery under deletion closure]
\label{cor:delete}
Assume in addition that the query family contains $q_{C \setminus \{i\}}$ for some $i \in C$, that it assigns the same polarity to every condition the two queries share, and that both queries satisfy the hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:back}.
Fix any reference $x_0 \in S$ with $x_0 \models C$; at least one exists, since every triple contains a positive.
Then $z_i$ is $(2\delta, S)$-linearly recovered by the probe
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-probe}
    b_i = s_i \big( f(q_C) - f(q_{C \setminus \{i\}}) \big) ,
    \qquad
    \beta_i = z_i(x_0) - \inner{b_i}{f(x_0)} .
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Applying Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-agg} to $q_C$ and to $q_{C \setminus \{i\}}$ in turn,
\begin{equation}
    \Big| \inner{f(q_C)}{\Delta f} - \sum_{j \in C} s_j \Delta z_j \Big| < \delta ,
    \qquad
    \Big| \inner{f(q_{C \setminus \{i\}})}{\Delta f} - \sum_{j \in C \setminus \{i\}} s_j \Delta z_j \Big| < \delta .
\end{equation}
Because the two queries agree in polarity on their shared conditions, the two sums differ in exactly the single term $s_i \Delta z_i$.
Subtracting the second bound from the first and applying the triangle inequality, together with linearity of the inner product in its first argument,
\begin{equation}
    \Big| \inner{f(q_C) - f(q_{C \setminus \{i\}})}{\Delta f} - s_i \Delta z_i \Big| < 2\delta .
\end{equation}
Multiplying the quantity inside the absolute value by $s_i$ leaves the value unchanged, since $|s_i| = 1$, and $s_i^2 = 1$ reduces $s_i \cdot s_i \Delta z_i$ to $\Delta z_i$, giving
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-diff}
    \big| \inner{b_i}{\Delta f} - \Delta z_i \big| < 2\delta
    \qquad \text{for every triple.}
\end{equation}
It remains to fix the offset.
The hypothesis of Theorem~\ref{thm:back} ranges over every triple with $x^+, x^- \in S$ and $x^+ \models C$, so we may take $x^+ = x_0$ and let $x^- = x$ range over all of $S$.
For such a pair Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-diff} reads
\begin{equation}
    \big| \inner{b_i}{f(x_0)} - \inner{b_i}{f(x)} - z_i(x_0) + z_i(x) \big| < 2\delta .
\end{equation}
By the definition of $\beta_i$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-probe} we have $\inner{b_i}{f(x_0)} - z_i(x_0) = -\beta_i$, so the quantity inside is $-\big( \inner{b_i}{f(x)} + \beta_i - z_i(x) \big)$, whose absolute value is unchanged by the sign.
Hence $\big| \inner{b_i}{f(x)} + \beta_i - z_i(x) \big| < 2\delta$ for all $x \in S$, which is Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-rec} with $\epsilon = 2\delta$.
\end{proof}

{\color{red}% unit-norm remark
\begin{remark}[Unit-norm embeddings]
\label{rem:unitnorm}
Suppose the embeddings are constrained to unit norm, $\|f(\ell)\| = 1$ for all $\ell \in L$, and the objective regresses onto the scaled targets $y/V$.
Let $U = \operatorname{span}\{f(x) : x \in S\}$ and assume $U \neq \mathbb{R}^d$.
Replacing each $b_i$ by its projection onto $U$ preserves Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-rec}, and if $V \ge \big\|\sum_{i \in C} s_i b_i\big\|$ then
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:app-unitq}
    f(q_C) = \frac{1}{V} \Big( \sum_{i \in C} s_i b_i + w \Big) ,
    \qquad
    w \perp U ,
    \quad
    \|w\|^2 = V^2 - \Big\| \sum_{i \in C} s_i b_i \Big\|^2 ,
\end{equation}
is a unit vector whose margins are those of Theorem~\ref{thm:forward} divided by $V$; the proof of the theorem then gives $\mathcal{L} < (2k\epsilon/V)^2$ against the targets $y/V$.

\end{remark}

\paragraph{Glossary.}
\begin{description}\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
    \item[$L$] Input set, the queries and documents composed of sequences of tokens. Not to be
    confused with the loss $\mathcal{L}$.
    \item[$S$] The subset of $L$ on which recovery is required; here the documents appearing in
    training triples. Easy negatives are excluded.
    \item[$f$] The embedding $f : L \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$. The analysis imposes no norm
    constraint; the deployed encoder normalizes its outputs.
    \item[$d$] Embedding dimension.
    \item[$x$] A document. $x^+$ is a positive, satisfying every condition of the query; $x^-$ is a
    hard negative drawn from the same annotated product pair.
    \item[$z_i$] Attribute indicator $z_i : L \rightarrow \{0,1\}$, equal to $1$ when the text has
    attribute $i$. Depends only on the text.
    \item[$m$] Total number of attributes in the corpus.
    \item[$q_C$] Query with condition set $C$.
    \item[$c_i$] A single condition, naming attribute $i$ together with a polarity.
    \item[$C$] The set of conditions of a query.
    \item[$k$] Number of conditions, $|C|$. The label depends only on discriminating conditions,
    but error bounds use the full $|C|$.
    \item[$k'$] Maximum number of attributes present in a single document; document-side
    sparsity, used only in Corollary~\ref{cor:capacity}.
    \item[$s_i$] Polarity of condition $c_i$; $+1$ when the query requires attribute $i$ to be
    present, $-1$ when it requires it absent. Always $|s_i| = 1$.
    \item[$b_i$] Linear probe vector for attribute $i$. A property of the attribute, not of the
    query: one probe serves both polarities.
    \item[$\beta_i$] Scalar offset of the probe for attribute $i$, fixed by a reference document.
    Equivalent to one extra coordinate, see Definition~\ref{def:rec}.
    \item[$M$] The margin $\inner{f(q_C)}{f(x^+) - f(x^-)}$, the quantity the model predicts.
    \item[$y$] The label, the number of conditions the negative violates.
    \item[$\mathcal{L}$] The loss $(M - y)^2$ on a triple.
    \item[$\epsilon$] Probe error: the tolerance in the recovery condition Eq.~\eqref{eq:app-rec}.
    \item[$\delta$] Margin error: the square root of the loss achieved on every triple.
    \item[$V$] Fixed label scale applied by the trainer. Absorbable for the unconstrained
    objective, and set to $1$ in the analysis.
    \item[$\ind{\cdot}$] Indicator, $1$ when its argument holds and $0$ otherwise.
    \item[$\models$] Satisfaction: $x \models c_i$ when document $x$ meets condition $c_i$, and
    $x \models C$ when it meets all of them.
\end{description}
